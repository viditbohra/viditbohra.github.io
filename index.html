Human–Object Interaction Retargeting

Research · GV Lab, University of Tokyo · Guide: Prof. Gentiane Venture · Jun – Jul 2026

An end-to-end pipeline that turns an ordinary monocular RGB video of a person handling an object into motion a robot can actually execute. Human pose comes from HMR2 (4D-Humans), joint positions are extracted through SMPL, and the motion is retargeted onto the robot with inverse kinematics in Holosoma. It runs on both a Pepper humanoid and a UR5 arm.

The interesting part is what it preserves. Copying joint angles across bodies of different proportions breaks the task — the hand ends up in the wrong place relative to the object. So the retargeting uses an interaction-mesh formulation that encodes the spatial relationship between the human and the object and preserves it through the transfer, keeping the manipulation semantics intact rather than just the pose.

Mars Rover Team — 5-DOF Robotic Arm

Mars Rover Team, IIT Bombay · Guide: Prof. P. J. Guruprasad · Oct 2024 – present

The manipulator on a semi-autonomous rover built by a 30-person team to cross rough terrain and perform dexterous tasks at international competitions. I designed the 5-DOF arm and reworked most of its drivetrain: a custom two-stage 81:1 cycloidal drive for torque density, explicit steering for cleaner control, and a 2-DOF differential wrist redesigned as a 3D print that came out 35 % lighter and far easier to manufacture.

Two failure modes drove the rest of the design. The scaled-up linear base kept binding on tolerance stack-up, so the rails were redesigned around the principle used in optical disk drives to take the over-constraint out. And because a power loss would let the wrist drop under its own weight, a worm gear drive holds position with no power applied.

With this rover the team placed 2nd at the European Rover Challenge (24 teams, 15 countries), took 1st in the Astrobiology Mission at the International Rover Challenge, and finished 9th overall in the IRC against 35+ institutions worldwide.

  • Cycloidal drive

Robo Sapien — Bipedal Robot

Institute Technical Summer Project (ITSP), IIT Bombay · May 2025 – present

A 6-DOF biped built from scratch after a literature review of existing walking robots set the baseline requirements. The frame uses lightweight carbon fibre tubes to keep mass down, limit sensors for joint homing, and a rack-and-pinion assembly that shifts the centre of mass predictably instead of relying on the legs alone to stay upright.

Control was approached from three directions. A PPO policy trained in Isaac Lab with a multi-term reward — velocity tracking, feet air time, joint limit penalties — produced a stable walking gait in simulation. On hardware, inverse kinematics and PID loops on an Arduino Mega with an MPU6050 handle real-time balance. Alongside those, a state-space model fitted from Simscape (85 % fit) drove a data-driven controller in Simulink, and an LQR controller on a linear inverted pendulum model produced a stable gait analytically.

  • PPO
  • Simulink / Simscape
  • LQR
  • Arduino
  • FDM printing

State Estimation on Lie Groups & Visual-Inertial Odometry

SC651, Prof. Ravi Banavar, IIT Bombay · Aug 2025 – present

This started as coursework in estimation on Lie groups: implementing the Kalman filter, TRIAD and QUEST on an IMU to get a stable attitude estimate, fusing gyroscope, accelerometer and magnetometer data. I then implemented the Mahony filter and compared it systematically against TRIAD and QUEST, which gave a 40 % lower standard deviation in the output.

It has since grown into a visual-inertial setup on hardware I built — an RP2040 Pico with an IMX335 camera and an MPU6050, characterised with an Allan deviation analysis to pin down the gyro and accelerometer noise parameters. The filter estimates the camera trajectory and a landmark map together from the fused visual and inertial stream.

  • Lie groups
  • Kalman / Mahony / TRIAD / QUEST
  • Allan deviation
  • Python
  • RP2040

Support-Free 3D Printing via Conical Slicing

Course project · ME230, Prof. Ramesh Singh · Jan – Apr 2026

Printing a 90° overhang normally means printing supports, then cutting them off and cleaning up the scar they leave. This project prints them without any support at all — on an unmodified 3-axis FDM printer, no hardware changes.

The trick is to do the work in software. The STL is pre-warped, sliced with an ordinary planar slicer, and then the resulting G-code is put through a back-transformation in Python, so the nozzle traces conical non-planar layers that support themselves as they build. The result: 25 % less material, 20 % shorter build time, and no post-processing at all.

  • Python
  • G-code
  • Non-planar slicing
  • FDM
  • SolidWorks

Stress Concentration Analysis via Photoelasticity

Course project · ME218 Solid Mechanics Lab, Prof. Sripriya Ramamoorthy · Jan – Apr 2026

Measuring stress concentration factors optically rather than numerically. Epoxy specimens were cast in TPU moulds with three hole geometries — square, filleted-square and elliptical — then loaded in a polariscope, where the isochromatic fringe pattern makes the stress field directly visible. Counting fringe order around the hole gives the SCF.

The measured values were validated against ANSYS FEA and agreed closely on where the concentrations appear. Filleting the sharp corners of the square hole cut the SCF by 21 %, and switching to an elliptical hole reduced it a further 33 %.

  • Photoelasticity
  • ANSYS FEA
  • Experimental mechanics
  • TPU moulding
